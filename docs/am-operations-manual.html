Client Health Score System

At 33+ clients per AM, gut feel is not a strategy. The Health Score replaces intuition with data and tells you exactly which clients need attention today.

The 5 Dimensions (100 Points Total)

Dimension Points What to Measure Red Flags
Satisfaction 25 Client happiness with VA & relationship Complaints, silence, vague responses
Utilization 20 Hours used vs hours paid Dramatically under-using hours
Billing 20 On-time payment, no disputes Late payments, billing holds
Engagement 20 Responsive, proactive communication Ghosting, declining communication
Growth 15 Expansion signals, referrals No growth conversation

Scoring Tiers & Action Triggers

🟢 GREEN (70-100)
Healthy and engaged. Monitor monthly. Look for expansion opportunities.
🟡 YELLOW (50-69)
Watch closely. Schedule check-in this week. Identify root cause.
🔴 RED (0-49)
URGENT. Client at churn risk. Call them TODAY. Escalate to Client Success Manager immediately. Develop intervention plan.

How to Calculate Scores (Weekly)

Scoring Procedure
1
Review Last 2 Weeks of Communication
Check Slack channels, Fathom call notes, email tone. Rate satisfaction 0-25 based on sentiment.
2
Check Utilization in Client Account Tracker
Compare hours used to hours contracted. Rate 0-20 based on usage percentage.
3
Verify Billing Status
Is current month paid? Any disputes or holds? Rate 0-20 accordingly.
4
Assess Engagement Level
Did they respond to outreach? Are they initiating contact? Rate 0-20.
5
Look for Growth Signals
Expansion requests, positive future signals, referral potential? Rate 0-15.
6
Update & Document
Record scores in Client Account Tracker. Add notes explaining any changes.

Client Management & Communication

Your clients are the revenue. Your job is to keep them satisfied, engaged, and growing. This section covers the tactical approach to client interaction.

Communication Principles

  • Warm check-ins for clients you've already engaged with (reference prior calls)
  • Fresh introductions for new clients on your roster (no assumption of prior contact)
  • Concise & clear: 3 sentences max, no em-dashes (use commas)
  • Always include calendar link for scheduling calls with Client Success Manager
  • Proactive > Reactive: Reach out before problems arise
  • Regular touchpoints: Minimum 2x per month
  • Same-day response: Client reaches out → you reply today

Client Outreach Process

Before Sending Any Outreach
1
Check Slack Posting History
Have you or another AM engaged with this client? Look for prior conversations.
2
Check Fathom for Call Attendance
Have you spoken with them? Did Jason do an intro call? This determines your tone.
3
Decide: Warm Check-in or Fresh Intro
Prior contact? → Warm check-in. No contact? → Fresh introduction.
4
Draft Message
Use templates below. Keep it to 3 sentences. Include Client Success Manager calendar link.
5
Send in Direct Message (Not Channel)
Slack DM directly to client contact. More personal, not public.
6
Document
Add note in Slack thread or Fathom notes documenting your outreach.

Client Communication Templates

Warm Check-in (Client You Know):
"Hi [Client], wanted to check in and see how [VA Name] is supporting you. Any feedback or areas we can improve? 📅 [Calendar Link]"
Fresh Introduction (New Client):
"Hi [Client], I'm [Your Name], your Account Manager at Goldbar. Excited to work together and support your growth! Let's connect soon. 📅 [Calendar Link]"
Quick Win (After Delivery):
"Hi [Client], [VA Name] just completed [specific task]. Let us know if you need anything else!"

VA Management & Development

Your VAs are your most important asset. Your job is to hire, onboard, coach, and develop them into operators who think strategically about their clients' businesses.

VA Coaching Philosophy

VAs should think like OPERATORS, not just assistants. This means: researching client businesses proactively, identifying improvement opportunities, making strategic recommendations, solving problems independently, and contributing to client growth. Your job is to coach them toward this mindset through feedback, training, and high expectations.

VA Assessment Framework

Evaluate each VA on these five dimensions:

Dimension What to Assess Red Flags Action Path
Reliability Attendance, punctuality, consistency Frequent absences, late starts, missed deadlines Coaching 1:1 or performance plan
Quality Work output, attention to detail, client feedback Client complaints, poor work, rework needed Training or skill-building with Training team
Communication Responsiveness, clarity, proactivity Slow responses, vague updates, not initiating Communication coaching or escalation
Ownership Takes initiative, solves problems, doesn't blame Always blames client, waits for direction Operator mindset coaching or restaffing discussion
Learning Improves over time, accepts feedback, grows Same mistakes repeating, defensive, stagnant Structured improvement plan or transition

Goldie Platform: Attendance & Performance Tracking

Goldy.ai is the operational platform where VAs track their time, submit work, and report on daily activities. You use it to monitor attendance, hours, and performance.

Daily Goldie Check (5-10 minutes)

Every morning, check Goldie for:

  • Attendance Status: Is each VA checked in? Are they active?
  • Any Absences?: Mark as: In / Out-Approved / Out-Pending / Absent / Missing
  • Hours Yesterday: Did they work their full shift? Any gaps?
  • BOD Reports: Did VAs submit their start-of-day updates?
  • Client Feedback: Any notes from clients about VA performance?

Leave Codes & Meanings

Code Meaning Duration Who Approves
UL Unpaid Leave Any length You (AM)
PL Paid Leave/Vacation Accrued hours You + Finance
SL Sick Leave As needed You (with note)
HOL Holiday Full day Auto-marked
OFF Scheduled Day Off Full day Set in schedule

Red Flags to Watch

  • Repeated Absences: More than 2 unscheduled absences per month = performance issue
  • Late Clock-ins: Consistently starting 5+ min late = reliability concern
  • Missing EOD Reports: Not logging out or submitting end-of-day = accountability issue
  • Hours Mismatch: Clock hours don't match contracted = time theft or burnout
  • Client Complaints: Red flag for immediate coaching needed
  • Incomplete Tasks: Tasks stuck in progress = needs help or capacity issue

Escalation Path for Goldie Issues

Attendance Issue (1-2 times)? → 1:1 conversation with VA

Attendance Issue (3+ times)? → Document in Goldie + escalate to Client Success Manager

Performance complaint from client? → Same-day 1:1 to understand + coach

Quality declining? → Schedule training with Training team

Chronic issues after coaching? → Escalate restaffing discussion to CSM

Escalation Protocol & Risk Management

Escalations are your early warning system. Learn to spot problems early, escalate quickly, and solve them systematically.

Three Escalation Tiers

Tier Severity Examples Action Timeline
Tier 1 Minor Small quality issue, minor delay, communication gap You handle + document Resolve within 48 hours
Tier 2 Medium VA performance problem, client dissatisfaction, scope creep You + CSM align on plan Escalate within 24 hours
Tier 3 Critical Client threatening cancel, major quality failure, billing dispute CSM takes lead, you support SAME-DAY response

Escalation Protocol (Step-by-Step)

How to Handle Any Escalation
1
Identify the Issue EARLY
Don't wait for client complaint. Spot problems from Slack, Fathom, Goldie, internal signals.
2
Document Clearly
What's the problem? Root cause? Impact on client/VA? Be specific, not vague.
3
Propose a Solution
Don't just report problems. Come with 1-2 recommendations to fix it.
4
Escalate Immediately
Reach out to Client Success Manager SAME DAY. If Tier 3, call immediately.
5
Get Approval on Plan
"Here's what I recommend, do you agree?" Don't assume you can execute alone.
6
Communicate to Client/VA
"Here's what we're doing to fix this." Transparency builds trust.
7
Follow Up & Track
Ensure resolution. Document outcome. Update your tracker.

Common Escalation Triggers

  • Client threatening to cancel
  • VA performance issues (attendance, quality, communication)
  • Billing disputes or payment holds
  • Communication breakdown between client & VA
  • Scope creep (hours exceeding allocation)
  • Missed deadlines or quality failures
  • Client control/delegation issues
  • Restaffing needed (fit issue or client request)

Restaffing Decision Tree

  1. Is this a coaching issue?
    Try 1:1 support & training first. Don't jump to restaffing.
  2. Is this a personality/fit issue?
    Are the VA and client fundamentally misaligned? Hard to coach?
  3. Have we invested in training?
    Send VA to Training team (Emma) for skill-building first.
  4. Does the client want the VA gone?
    If yes → escalate restaffing discussion to CSM immediately.
  5. Finance approval needed?
    If expansion needed, send request to Rowan BEFORE talking to Staffing.
  6. Discuss with Staffing Team
    Once CSM approves → talk to Gabriella (Staffing Lead) about replacement.
  7. Manage the transition carefully
    New VA = new expectations. Set them up for success.
